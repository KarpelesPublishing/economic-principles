# Chapter 4: Opportunity Cost, Specialization, Scale, Scope, and Technology

Count what a choice gives up, and let the size of the market decide how to produce.

Notebook with the worked examples and figures: `../../notebooks/ch004-opportunity-cost-specialization-scale-scope-and-technology.ipynb`

## 1. Le Chatelier principle (le-chatelier-principle)

    q = 10 sqrt(LK);  L_SR = 291,600/270 = 1,080;  L_LR = 540 sqrt(40/15) approx 882

Steps: short run, the fixed input pins the other (labor response zero); long run, use the tangency wL = rK with the output target. Expect larger long-run responses.

## 2. Smith extent-of-the-market theorem (smith-extent-of-the-market-theorem)

    q* = F / (c_general - c_special - extra cost per unit)  (book: 24,000 / (90 - 30) = 400)

Steps: compare F + (c_special + extra) q with c_general q (book at 750 with delivery 3: 48,750 versus 67,500). Use reachable sales after delivery and border costs.

## 3. Division of labor and comparative advantage (comparative-advantage)

    OC_A(C) = 2/4 = 0.5W;  OC_B(C) = 6/8 = 0.75W

Steps: opportunity costs; each side specializes where its cost is lower; trade at a price between the two (book: 0.625); check each bundle lies beyond the producer's own frontier (book: 52 > 48 hours, 104 > 96).

## 4. Opportunity cost and household production (opportunity-cost)

    FC = cash + hours x v;  v* = (42 - 18)/(1.5 - 0.25) = 19.20

Steps: full cost of each option at the user's value of an hour; break-even v*. Value time at its best real alternative, which may be below the wage.
